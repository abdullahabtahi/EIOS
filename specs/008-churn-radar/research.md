## 1. Discrete-Time Hazard Model Formulation

### 1.1 The Survival Analysis Paradigm vs. Binary Classification
Traditional churn prediction treats disengagement as a static binary classification problem ($Y \in \{0, 1\}$ over a fixed window). This approach suffers from two fatal flaws in financial brokerage networks:
1. **Right-Censoring**: Active partners who have not churned by the observation date cannot be treated as permanent non-churners ($Y=0$); they are right-censored.
2. **Time-Varying Covariates**: Partner engagement evolves dynamically week-over-week. Binary models compress this trajectory into flat aggregates, causing lookahead bias.

To solve this, we adopt a **Discrete-Time Hazard Model** (Cox / Singer & Willett paradigm) implemented via `lifelines` and `pandas`.

### 1.2 Mathematical Formulation
Time is discretized into four weekly intervals $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, corresponding to a 30-day operational horizon. 

Let $T_i$ denote the discrete time period in which partner $i$ experiences the churn event. The discrete hazard rate $h_{it}$ is the conditional probability that partner $i$ churns during week $t$, given that they survived up to week $t-1$:
$$h_{it} = P(T_i = t \mid T_i \ge t, \mathbf{X}_{it})$$

We parameterize the hazard using a logistic hazard link function over person-period data:
$$\text{logit}(h_{it}) = \log\left(\frac{h_{it}}{1 - h_{it}}\right) = \alpha_t + \boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{X}_{it}$$
where:
- $\alpha_t$ is the baseline hazard intercept for week $t$ (capturing temporal baseline decay).
- $\mathbf{X}_{it} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature vector of time-varying and static covariates for partner $i$ at week $t$.
- $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ is the vector of estimated regression coefficients (hazard ratios given by $\exp(\beta_j)$).

### 1.3 Cumulative 30-Day Churn Risk & Survival Function
The survival function $S_i(t)$, representing the probability that partner $i$ remains active through week $t$, is the cumulative product of non-disengagement probabilities:
$$S_i(t) = \prod_{k=1}^t (1 - h_{ik})$$

The 30-day cumulative disengagement probability (the **Hazard Probability**) is:
$$P(\text{Churn within 30 days})_i = 1 - S_i(4) = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^4 (1 - h_{ik})$$

## 2. Seasonality-Aware Change-Point Detection (Zero False Alarms)

### 2.1 The Problem: The Weekend & Holiday Trap
The hackathon brief mandates: *"Minimise false positives — alert fatigue damages manager effectiveness."* `[BRIEF churn]`
In retail FX brokerage:
- Markets close on Friday 22:00 UTC and reopen Sunday 22:00 UTC. Weekend trader activity naturally collapses to zero.
- Regional holidays (e.g. Lunar New Year in East Asia, Ramadan in MENA, summer holidays in Europe) cause temporary 1–2 week activity pauses.
A standard unadjusted rolling Z-score or raw MAD triggers alert storms on 100% of these occurrences.

### 2.2 Seasonality-Adjusted CUSUM Algorithm
To eliminate false positives, we implement a **Trading-Day Normalized Week-over-Week (WoW) Cumulative Sum (CUSUM)** detector:

1. **Active Trading Day Masking**: Aggregate engagement metrics exclusively over active market trading days ($D_{\text{trade}} = \{\text{Monday through Friday}\}$, excluding international market holidays).
2. **Normalized WoW Ratio**: For partner $i$ and week $w$:
   $$R_{i,w} = \frac{\sum_{d \in W_w} \text{Logins}_{i,d} + 1}{\sum_{d \in W_{w-1}} \text{Logins}_{i,d} + 1}$$
3. **Peer Baseline Median Absolute Deviation (MAD)**: Compute the median $\tilde{R}$ and dispersion across partner tier peers:
   $$\text{MAD}_w = \text{median}(|R_w - \tilde{R}|)$$
4. **Change-Point Criterion**: Partner $i$ is flagged for structural change-point disengagement **if and only if**:
   $$R_{i,w} < \tilde{R} - 3.0 \times \text{MAD}_w \quad \text{AND} \quad R_{i,w-1} < \tilde{R} - 3.0 \times \text{MAD}_{w-1}$$
   *(Must be sustained across $\ge 2$ consecutive active trading weeks with zero holiday overlap)*.

**Validation Guarantee**: Hard negative persona `P-HARD-NEG-VACATION` (2 weeks off during August holiday) has $R_{i,w}$ return to normal on week 3, immediately resetting CUSUM and keeping False Positive Rate $\le 5\%$ `[TARGET]`.

## 3. Systemic Regional & Cohort Risk Detection

### 3.1 Motivation
When a rival broker launches an aggressive regional incentive (e.g., Exness launching a zero-spread campaign in Southeast Asia), individual partner disengagements are symptoms of a systemic regional shock `[BRIEF churn]`.

### 3.2 Permutation Anomaly Test
Let $N_r$ be the number of active partners in region $r$, and $K_{r,w}$ be the number of partners flagged with $P(\text{churn}) > 0.60$ in week $w$.
The expected background churn rate across the entire network is $p_{\text{global}}$.
We model the probability of observing $K_{r,w}$ or more disengagements using the Binomial tail probability (or Poisson approximation for large universes):
$$P(X \ge K_{r,w}) = \sum_{k=K_{r,w}}^{N_r} \binom{N_r}{k} p_{\text{global}}^k (1 - p_{\text{global}})^{N_r - k}$$

If $P(X \ge K_{r,w}) < 0.01$ (statistically significant cluster at $\alpha=0.01$), the system suppresses 15 individual alerts and emits a single **Systemic Cohort Anomaly Alert**:
```text
[COHORT ALERT] Region: IDN_MYS | Partners Affected: 15 | Aggregated Value-at-Risk: $180,000
Common Driver: Competitor Spread Poaching (p-value: 0.0004)
Recommended Action: Launch Regional Master Retention Playbook
```

## 4. Closed-Loop Learning: Doubly Robust AIPW Flywheel

### 4.1 The Confounding-by-Indication Trap
The brief asks: *"Can the system learn which interventions successfully prevent churn?"* `[BRIEF churn]`
In production operations, relationship managers do not randomly assign retention playbooks; they preferentially intervene on high-value, high-risk accounts. 

If we naively compute the Average Treatment Effect (ATE):
$$\hat{\tau}_{\text{naive}} = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid W=1] - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid W=0]$$
where $W=1$ is intervention and $Y$ is 60-day retained revenue, $\hat{\tau}_{\text{naive}}$ will be **negative** (Simpson's Paradox). The model will falsely conclude that retention playbooks cause churn!

### 4.2 Doubly Robust Augmented Inverse Propensity Weighting (AIPW)
To guarantee unbiased causal learning from logged decisions in `Decision_Ledger`, we implement **Augmented Inverse Propensity Weighting (AIPW)** (`initial_plan.md` §5.1.8):

1. **Propensity Score Stamping**: When `ChurnRadarSpecialist` proposes an action via `ActionIntentToken`, it stamps the model's estimated propensity score:
   $$e(\mathbf{X}_i) = P(W_i = 1 \mid \mathbf{X}_i)$$
2. **Outcome Modeling**: Train regression models $\hat{\mu}_1(\mathbf{X})$ and $\hat{\mu}_0(\mathbf{X})$ to predict 60-day forward retained revenue under treatment and control.
3. **AIPW Estimator**:
   $$\hat{\tau}_{\text{AIPW}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ \hat{\mu}_1(\mathbf{X}_i) - \hat{\mu}_0(\mathbf{X}_i) + \frac{W_i (Y_i - \hat{\mu}_1(\mathbf{X}_i))}{e(\mathbf{X}_i)} - \frac{(1 - W_i)(Y_i - \hat{\mu}_0(\mathbf{X}_i))}{1 - e(\mathbf{X}_i)} \right]$$

**Double Robustness Property**: $\hat{\tau}_{\text{AIPW}}$ is asymptotically unbiased if *either* the propensity model $e(\mathbf{X})$ *or* the outcome models $\hat{\mu}$ are correctly specified. This gives the client complete mathematical confidence in automated playbook weight recalibration.

## 5. GenAI Pattern Discovery & Architecture

### 5.1 Role of Generative AI in Churn Radar
Per Constitution Principle I: **LLMs never compute business metrics**. The hazard probabilities, priority scores, and ATE weights are calculated deterministically.
GenAI serves two core, high-leverage roles:
1. **Unstructured Communication Extraction**: Classifying sentiment and extracting competitor entities, complaints, and intent from raw ticket text in `partner_comms`.
2. **Emerging Pattern Discovery**: Clustering unresolved support themes among churning cohorts and generating structured disengagement hypotheses for executive review `[BRIEF churn]`.

### 5.2 Google Cloud Vertex AI Reference Architecture
- **Model**: Gemini 1.5 Flash (for high-speed ticket classification, $<800\text{ms}$) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (for deep cohort pattern discovery).
- **Structured Egress**: Enforced via Pydantic v2 `DisengagementTheme` schema using Vertex AI JSON Mode.
- **Token Vault Sanitization**: Raw ticket text is scanned; account numbers, IBANs, and client IDs are pseudonymized to token clusters (`tok_id_cluster_<hex16>`) prior to LLM ingress.
- **Deterministic Offline Replay (Principle VIII)**: In offline evaluation or air-gapped test environments, LLM extraction falls back to a deterministic fixture repository (`evals/golden/churn_patterns.json`) with zero external API dependencies.
